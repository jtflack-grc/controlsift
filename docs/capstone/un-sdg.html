    Reduced Inequalities: rubric label vs. UN designation

    The MMC capstone rubric labels Reduced Inequalities as program option 4. In the official United Nations Sustainable Development Goals, Reduced Inequalities is SDG 10. ControlSift therefore uses both labels together: MMC rubric option 4 / official UN SDG 10.

    Why ControlSift fits the theme

    MMC option 4 / UN SDG 10

    Unequal access to cybersecurity capacity

    NIST explicitly notes that smaller businesses often operate with limited cybersecurity resources and budgets and may be unable to afford dedicated cybersecurity staff. ControlSift does not claim that this proves a specific shortage of control-evidence reviewers; it uses the broader resource constraint as motivation to study an open, low-cost assurance-support method.

    Contribution

    Lower the research barrier, not the assurance standard

    The project uses synthetic data, an inexpensive classical baseline, a small language model, public code, free or commodity GPU infrastructure, and explicit human-review boundaries. The goal is inspectable research access, not a cheap substitute for professional judgment.

    Public evidence behind the framing

    • NIST Small Business Cybersecurity: limited resources, budgets, and staffing are real constraints. Source
    • NIST small-business team guidance: discusses the challenge of affording dedicated staff and the use of outsourcing, upskilling, and community support. Source
    • NIST SP 800-53A Rev. 5: grounds the distinction between document presence and evidence that controls are implemented and producing intended outcomes. Source
    • United Nations Goal 10: confirms that Reduced Inequalities is officially SDG 10. Source

    Research boundaryClassical experiments use dataset v1.1.0. Gemma experiments use v1.0.0. Cross-version scores are published for transparency but do not support a controlled classical-vs-Gemma ranking.

    Within Gemma v1.0, few-shot is strongest and QLoRA does not beat it. The hardened v1.1 classical benchmark has a TF-IDF baseline around 0.53 macro F1. The capstone contribution is not a model victory; it is an open, low-cost, inspectable research process with honest limits and failure visibility.
