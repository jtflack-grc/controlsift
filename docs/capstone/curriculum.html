    Course 01 · Google Skills

    Build Your Own Small Language Model

    complete

    Public intent: language-model fundamentals, the ML pipeline, n-gram vs transformer limits, research-engineer practice, and a community-relevant problem framed responsibly.

    Introduction to the language modeling problem

    The power of language models

    meets ControlSift is an applied LM research project: can a small instruction-tuned model judge evidence quality? The research site and paper state why LMs are relevant here without claiming production audit automation.

    Predict the next word / role of probabilities

    meets Classification is framed as constrained next-token generation over five labels. Temperature 0.0 inference is specified so sampling noise does not inflate variance. Majority baseline (macro F1 0.067) makes class-prior probability explicit before any model claim.

    Lab: Create your own probability distribution

    meets Balanced five-class design plus majority-class baseline quantify the chance floor. Per-class F1 and confusion matrices show where probability mass concentrates for classical models (e.g. CONTRADICTORY vs SUFFICIENT).

    From n-grams to transformers

    N-grams and language patterns

    meets TF-IDF + logistic regression is the explicit bag-of-words / n-gram-family baseline on the same sealed splits—ControlSift’s stand-in for “what lexical patterns alone can do.”

    Lab: Experiment with n-grams

    meets scripts/run_tfidf_baseline.py trains and scores TF-IDF on train→test/challenge; results live under results/tfidf/ and the public Results page.

    Limitations of n-grams

    meets Early draft data let TF-IDF saturate (label-unique phrases). That was treated as a dataset failure, not success. v1.1 dual-section packing + adversarial surface harden restored a meaningful ceiling (~0.53 macro F1), proving lexical shortcuts are insufficient for the intended judgment task.

    How research engineers approach real problems

    meets Protocol seal before LLM claims, family-isolated splits, Failure Lab, structural + scripted audits, and machine-readable metrics only—research-engineer discipline rather than demo theater.

    Values, culture, and ethical choice

    meets Project values are explicit: honesty over polish, human judgment remains authoritative, no fabricated metrics, no employer data, no production-auditor replacement. Documented in Intended Use, AGENTS.md, and SDG framing (strong institutions via accountable evidence triage).

    Transformer models

    Compare n-gram models and transformer language models

    meets Ladder is majority → TF-IDF → Gemma zero-shot → few-shot → QLoRA. Full TEST ladder published (TF-IDF leads; few-shot best of Gemma; QLoRA did not beat few-shot).

    Anatomy of a language model

    meets Model Card and configs name Gemma 3 1B IT, quantization, LoRA rank/alpha, sequence length, and inference settings—enough anatomy for an applied PEFT study without reimplementing a toy SLM.

    What it means to train a model

    meets Training means supervised adaptation on sealed train/validation with fixed seed, frozen prompt, and held-out test/challenge never used for tuning. Early stopping / eval steps are in the QLoRA config.

    Values framework for LLM development

    meets Assurance hub + risk register + limitations act as the operational values framework: what may be claimed, what is prohibited, and who is protected.

    Training a model

    Machine learning development pipeline

    meets End-to-end pipeline: generate → validate → seal → classical baselines → Gemma baselines → QLoRA → aggregate evaluation → Failure Lab → public Results. Documented in Methods and Reproduce.

    Prepare the dataset for training

    meets Deterministic generator, JSONL splits, validators, manifest hashes, protocol seal tag protocol-v1-locked.

    Train a small language model / evaluate performance

    partial ControlSift adapts a 1B instruction-tuned model rather than training an SLM from scratch (the from-scratch pedagogy is covered by classical baselines + PEFT workflow). Evaluation uses macro F1, accuracy, per-class scores, and challenge set.

    Challenge

    Anticipating benefits

    meets Intended benefit: faster, more honest triage for human reviewers—not autopilot audits. SDG 16 articulates institutional accountability upside; Limitations and Risk Register bound downside.

    Develop your problem statement

    meets Published problem statement and research aim on Problem & impact and in the research paper: distinguish proof from paperwork on a sealed five-class synthetic benchmark.

    Artifacts: Paper · Problem & impact · Results · Protocol

    Course 02 · Google Skills

    Represent Your Language Data

    complete

    Public intent: prepare and represent text for LMs (tokenization, embeddings), critique bias/exclusion in data work, and design datasets ethically with Data Cards.

    Introduction to text data

    Teaching a machine the “soul” of language / types and sources of text

    meets ControlSift uses synthetic compositional evidence packets (not scraped binders). RESEARCH_SURFACE.md states what the text is and is not—compositional structure vs naturalistic OCR—so meaning and limits are not smuggled past the reader.

    Exploring raw data

    meets Dataset exploration notebook, processed JSONL, Failure Lab examples, and Data Card composition fields expose raw case shape to reviewers.

    Ownership, consent, privacy, exclusion

    meets Synthetic organizations only; no employer/customer evidence; no private logs. Privacy and non-extraction are hard scope rules in AGENTS.md and the Data Card.

    Preprocessing

    Lab: Preprocess data

    meets Generator emits normalized dual-section packets with shared SCOPE/ROW_DETAIL scaffolding, substance pointers, and decoys—deliberate preprocessing for hardness, not cosmetic cleaning.

    Data resources / who owns the data?

    meets All training text is project-generated under the repo license posture; there is no third-party proprietary evidence corpus to misappropriate. Provenance is rule-derived labels + audit trail.

    Tokenization

    Character / word / subword tokenization (incl. BPE)

    partial ControlSift does not reimplement a BPE trainer; it uses Gemma’s tokenizer at inference/training time and designs evidence text so token-level shortcuts alone cannot solve the task (shared decoys, dual sections). Lexical baseline tests the “word-ish” representation extreme.

    Whose voice is missing? / digital exclusion

    meets Scope honesty: English synthetic GRC packets are not global language coverage. Limitations and SDG 4 supporting claim are about open research craft, not pretending multilingual inclusion was achieved.

    Embeddings

    What embeddings are / properties of embeddings

    partial Classical path uses sparse TF-IDF vectors; neural path uses Gemma hidden states / LoRA-adapted representations. We do not ship a custom embedding visualization lab; we do ship Failure Lab cases that show when surface similarity misleads.

    Prepare a dataset for an SLM with a tokenizer

    meets Prompt templates format control + evidence into instruction turns compatible with Gemma 3 IT; max sequence length and packing constraints live in config.

    Challenge — ethical dataset design

    Why document data?

    meets Without documentation, synthetic packets could be mistaken for production binders or human gold labels. Documentation is mandatory for integrity.

    Build a dataset ethically with a Data Card

    meets Full Data Card: motivation, composition, labeling (rule-derived + audits), splits, risks, and intended use. Public HTML + governance markdown.

    Artifacts: Data Card · governance/RESEARCH_SURFACE.md · src/controlsift/data/generate.py · src/controlsift/prompting/

    Course 03 · Google Skills

    Design and Train Neural Networks

    complete

    Public intent: generalization, over/underfitting, MLP/training mechanics, hyperparameter discipline, alignment/safety awareness, and anticipation of social impacts.

    Signal and noise

    Signal vs noise in model performance

    meets Label-unique phrases were noise masquerading as signal. Removing them (v1.1 harden) forced models to use judgment-relevant structure. Failure Lab preserves hard cases where noise still wins for classical models.

    Overfitting and underfitting

    meets Perfect TF-IDF = overfit to leakage. Majority F1 0.067 = underfit / chance. Lexical ceiling test (tests/test_lexical_ceiling.py) guards against both extremes regressing.

    Generalization

    Bias–variance trade-off

    meets Macro F1 and per-class tables make class-wise bias visible (SUFFICIENT weak; CONTRADICTORY easier). Challenge set probes generalization beyond the main test slice.

    Training and test splits

    meets 1000/200/200/100 with scenario-family isolation—families do not cross partitions. Hashes sealed in PROTOCOL_SEAL.json.

    Loss curves / diagnostics

    meets Learning-curve script for classical models; QLoRA config defines eval/logging steps for training diagnostics once GPU runs.

    Anticipating risks

    meets Risk register written before LLM claims: false assurance, schema gaming, overclaiming human labels, metric fabrication.

    The multilayer perceptron

    Single-layer vs multilayer classification

    partial Logistic regression on TF-IDF is the linear classifier baseline; Gemma provides the deep nonlinear model. We do not reimplement an MLP-from-scratch lab in-repo because the applied ladder already spans linear → transformer PEFT.

    Non-linearly separable structure

    meets Dual-section packets with decoys intentionally create cases where a single lexical cue is insufficient—requiring compositional reading.

    Preventing overfitting and improving generalization

    Hyperparameters

    meets Documented in configs/: LoRA r/alpha/dropout, LR, epochs, batch/grad-accum, max seq length, seed 42. Classical baseline configs similarly pinned.

    Validation dataset / mitigate overfitting

    meets Held-out validation for model selection; test and challenge sealed. Protocol forbids tuning against the held-out test set.

    Alignment and safety (course module)

    meets Intended Use + prohibited production claims + parse constraints keep the system’s behavior tied to research goals—preview of Course 06, already enforced in governance.

    Gradients and backpropagation

    Gradient descent, backprop, optimizers (SGD/Adam)

    partial ControlSift does not re-derive JAX gradients; QLoRA training uses the standard PEFT/transformers stack (AdamW-family optimizer settings in config). The learning goal—parameters update from loss on labeled data—is the actual adaptation path.

    Challenge — anticipation

    Anticipating social impacts / impact statement

    meets Problem & impact, Risk Register, Limitations, and SDG pages are the impact statement: who benefits, who must not be automated away, what false assurance looks like.

    Artifacts: Failure Lab · Risk register · scripts/run_learning_curve.py · tests/test_lexical_ceiling.py

    Course 04 · Google Skills

    Discover the Transformer Architecture

    complete

    Public intent: attention and transformer blocks, positional information, generation trade-offs, community engagement, and stakeholder mapping.

    Introduction

    Architecture of modern LLMs / what drives predictions

    meets Base model is Gemma 3 1B Instruct—a modern decoder transformer. Frozen prompt design makes control statement + evidence the conditioning context for the label token(s).

    Attention weights (conceptual use of Gemma attention)

    partial We do not ship an attention-visualization notebook in v1; we do design packets so the model must integrate claim + distractor sections—the applied reason attention matters for this task. Failure Lab and per-class tables support applied error analysis.

    Community values shaping AI meaning

    meets Values here = institutional accountability and review integrity (SDG 16), not replacing auditors. Explicit non-users protected in stakeholder map.

    The attention mechanism

    Combining token information / masked & multi-head attention

    partial Pedagogy of implementing attention equations is covered by the curriculum labs; ControlSift’s meeting criterion is correct use of a multi-head transformer (Gemma) with architecture-aware LoRA module selection—not a from-scratch attention rewrite.

    Human meaning vs automated systems

    meets Intended Use: human judgment remains authoritative. Automation is triage research, not decision replacement—directly answering the “what may be lost” prompt.

    Assembling a transformer

    Positional embeddings / transformer blocks / MLP / layer norm

    partial Consumed via Gemma 3; Model Card + config document parameter-efficient adaptation points. Trainable LoRA parameters are the project’s explicit trainable subset.

    Trainable parameters in the transformer

    meets QLoRA freezes base weights (4-bit) and trains low-rank adapters; target_modules must be verified on load—documented as a required GPU step, not a blind copy from other families.

    Reflection and practice

    Pros and cons of transformers

    meets Pros: contextual reading of dual-section packets. Cons: compute need, schema-gaming risk, opaque errors—named in RESEARCH_SURFACE and Limitations.

    Decoding and generation

    meets Inference settings: temperature 0.0, do_sample false; robust label parsing; invalid outputs scored as errors—not remapped to a preferred class.

    Why engagement matters

    meets Public research site, Assurance docs, and MMC hub are the engagement surface for evaluators and RAI readers—not a silent private notebook.

    Challenge

    Mapping stakeholders and social values

    meets Problem & impact lists beneficiaries (human reviewers), readers (ML/RAI/residency), and protected parties (orgs that must not treat scores as audit conclusions).

    Mini-engagement plan

    meets Engagement plan = public Results + Failure Lab + Assurance + this hub + research paper; structural/scripted audits instead of fake “human gold.”

    Artifacts: Stakeholders · Model Card · configs/gemma3_1b_qlora.yaml

    Course 05 · Google Skills

    Fine-Tune Your Model

    complete

    Public intent: why fine-tune; task formatting; full-parameter vs LoRA/PEFT on Gemma-scale models; SFT limits vs RL overview; foresight and governance.

    Introduction to fine-tuning

    Importance of specialization / how will your model respond?

    meets Pretrained Gemma is not a control-evidence classifier out of the box. Zero-shot/few-shot legs exist specifically to measure the gap that fine-tuning must justify.

    Lab: Prompt your SLM with questions

    meets scripts/run_gemma_baseline.py and notebooks implement prompted classification; TEST metrics published (zero-shot 0.0796 · few-shot 0.1365).

    Gemma: fine-tuning for specific tasks

    meets Task is fixed: five-class evidence quality on sealed data. Model ID google/gemma-3-1b-it in config.

    Formatting

    Input/output formats / turn-based dialogue formatting

    meets Frozen instruction template turns control + evidence into a constrained classification prompt with parseable label space—same spirit as task formatting labs, domain-specific content.

    Full-parameter fine-tuning

    Advantages of fine-tuning / when to stop

    meets Hypotheses in the protocol compare adapted vs prompted models. Eval steps and epoch caps define stopping; test set remains sealed.

    Full-parameter fine-tuning of an SLM / of Gemma

    partial Full-parameter Gemma fine-tuning is intentionally not the v1 path (memory). We meet the learning goal by documenting why PEFT is chosen and by keeping classical + prompted baselines so PEFT gains are interpretable.

    Imagining AI in cultural contexts / narrative reflection

    meets Reflection page + research paper Limitations/RAI sections play the narrative/foresight role: what this tool would mean if overclaimed in GRC culture.

    Parameter-efficient fine-tuning

    Foundation models

    meets Gemma 3 1B IT is the foundation model; domain adaptation is the research question.

    Low-rank adaptation (LoRA) / implement LoRA / fine-tune Gemma3-1B with LoRA

    meets Exact course target: LoRA/QLoRA on Gemma 3 1B. TEST macro F1 0.0827 with parse success ~0.435 — did not beat few-shot; negative finding preserved.

    Opportunities and limitations of SFT

    Limits of supervised fine-tuning

    meets Disclosed: SFT on synthetic labels can teach schema reading; RESEARCH_SURFACE and error-analysis plan monitor that failure mode. Labels are rule-derived, not preference data.

    Reinforcement learning from human feedback (overview)

    partial ControlSift does not run RLHF/DPO. Meeting criterion: understand and state that preference optimization is out of v1 scope; alignment is handled via IT base + use constraints (Course 06).

    Challenge — foresight and governance

    Foresight and governance / governance blueprint

    meets Assurance hub is the blueprint: Protocol, Data Card, Model Card, Intended Use, Risk Register, Limitations, Human Review honesty, AGENTS.md non-negotiables.

    Artifacts: configs/gemma3_1b_qlora.yaml · scripts/run_gemma_qlora_eval.py · Assurance · Methods

    Course 06 · Official path (labs not yet in public GitHub tree)

    Align Your Model

    complete

    Public intent (path-level): align model behavior with intended goals, values, and safer use. ControlSift maps this as use-alignment and behavioral constraint for a research classifier—not as an RLHF training run.

    Intended goals and values

    Define what “good behavior” means for this system

    meets Good behavior = emit one of five labels consistently with sealed ground truth for research measurement; never present as an audit decision. Codified in Intended Use and Protocol hypotheses.

    Starting from an aligned base

    Instruction-tuned foundation model

    meets Gemma 3 1B IT already carries instruction-following priors; ControlSift does not start from a raw base checkpoint for v1.

    Behavioral constraints at inference

    Constrained outputs and robust parsing

    meets Label parser; invalid generations count as errors. No silent remapping to a preferred class when the model disagrees—preserves research integrity (AGENTS.md).

    Deterministic decoding for evaluation

    meets Temperature 0.0 / do_sample false reduces alignment-irrelevant sampling noise during scored runs.

    Risk reduction and refusal surface

    Prohibited uses and bright lines

    meets No production auditor claims; no secrets; no fabricated metrics; no test-set tuning; no describing synthetic evidence as production evidence.

    Risk register

    meets Documented failure modes (false assurance, schema gaming, overclaiming label provenance) with mitigation status.

    Preference optimization boundary

    SFT vs RLHF/DPO (what we do not claim)

    meets Explicit non-claim: v1 does not train a reward model or run preference optimization. Alignment work is governance + IT base + evaluation honesty—transparent about the method boundary.

    Human oversight

    Human judgment remains authoritative

    meets Stated on Intended Use, homepage posture, and capstone pages. Label audits are structural/scripted—never sold as large-scale human gold.

    Artifacts: Responsible innovation · Intended use · Risk register · AGENTS.md

    Course 07 · Google Skills

    Accelerate Your Model

    complete

    Public intent: single-GPU training, efficiency vs performance, FLOPs/memory estimation, bfloat16, gradient accumulation, environmental justice and a sustainability plan.

    Introduction

    Efficiency versus performance

    meets Chose 1B + QLoRA over larger full fine-tunes so the research question fits free-tier GPUs. Classical baselines remain so “faster/cheaper” never substitutes for measured quality.

    Lab: Compare models of different sizes

    partial Ladder compares method classes (majority / TF-IDF / prompted 1B / QLoRA 1B) rather than a multi-size Gemma sweep—size is fixed; method varies. Honest scope for v1 compute.

    GPU architecture (applied)

    meets Executed on a single free-tier GPU via packaged runner; metrics live on Results.

    Environmental impacts / stakeholder map of impacts

    meets Sustainability choices: smallest adequate model, free-tier, smoke before full, no multi-GPU cluster requirement. Stakeholders for compute impact = learner/researcher budget and shared free-tier queues.

    Compute

    Training vs inference compute / reducing compute

    meets 4-bit NF4 + LoRA reduces trainable and resident footprint; inference uses greedy decoding. Packaging separates smoke vs full runs to avoid wasted cycles.

    Lab: Estimate training FLOPs (applied discipline)

    partial No separate FLOPs notebook in-repo; compute humility is operationalized by model size choice, PEFT, and smoke tests rather than a FLOPs spreadsheet.

    Mapping carbon / developer role

    meets Documented preference for free-tier single-GPU and against unnecessary re-runs; metrics publish only from completed jobs—no vanity re-training.

    GPU memory

    Hitting memory walls / estimating GPU memory

    meets QLoRA + batch size 1 + grad accumulation 8 is the designed answer to the memory wall on free GPUs.

    Lab: Fine-tune with bfloat16

    meets Config sets bnb_4bit_compute_dtype: bfloat16 for the QLoRA path.

    Other considerations

    Gradient accumulation

    meets gradient_accumulation_steps: 8 with per-device batch 1—direct course technique, applied to Gemma 3 1B.

    Advanced scaling techniques

    partial v1 stops at QLoRA/single GPU; no FSDP/multi-node. Scope discipline is intentional.

    Challenge — sustainability plan

    AI and environmental justice / design a sustainability plan

    meets Plan: (1) 1B not 7B+/full FT, (2) 4-bit + LoRA, (3) smoke then full, (4) free-tier only, (5) seal data so failed runs are not repeated for leakage hunting. Written into KAGGLE_SAFE_RUN and Reproduce.

    Artifacts: scripts/package_for_kaggle.py · notebooks/KAGGLE_SAFE_RUN.md · notebooks/kaggle_runner.ipynb · Reproduce
