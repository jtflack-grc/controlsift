    Reflection

    Findings from the classical stage, questions reserved for Gemma evaluation, and limits of the current benchmark.

    Findings to date

    • Lexical shortcuts matter. An early generator revision let TF-IDF reach perfect macro F1; hardening the dataset brought the baseline to ~0.53 and restored a meaningful research question.
    • Compositional difficulty is uneven. CONTRADICTORY remains easier for classical models than SUFFICIENT and IRRELEVANT; per-class analysis is required alongside macro F1.
    • Protocol discipline works. Freezing prompt and split hashes before LLM claims keeps the evaluation contract stable.
    • Label provenance must stay explicit. Rule-derived labels with audit coverage are sufficient for this stage when disclosed accurately.

    Open questions (GPU stage)

    • How zero-shot Gemma performs on the sealed packet format
    • Whether few-shot prompting improves over zero-shot on test and challenge
    • Whether QLoRA yields a material, uncertainty-aware gain over prompting
    • Which error modes are new relative to the TF-IDF baseline

    Benchmark limits

    • Single-artifact synthetic packets, not multi-document binders or OCR noise
    • No organization-specific audit politics or negotiation context
    • No claim of large-scale human gold labeling
    • No live deployment study (out of v1 scope)

    Next steps

    • Run the Gemma ladder on free GPU and publish metrics from result files
    • Refresh the Failure Lab with model disagreements
    • Extend the research report with H1–H5 answers and uncertainty language
