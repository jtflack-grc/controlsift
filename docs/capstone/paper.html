ControlSift

    Can a Small Language Model Tell Proof from Paperwork?

    Domain adaptation of Gemma 3 for cybersecurity control-evidence assessment — with sealed evaluation, classical baselines, and responsible-AI governance.

    ControlSift Capstone Research Paper · Dataset v1.1.0 · Protocol protocol-v1-locked
    Mentor Me Collective · Google DeepMind AI Research Foundations
    Primary metric: macro F1 · Seed: 42 · Status: classical stage complete; LLM stage pending GPU execution

    Abstract

    Distinguishing operational proof from administrative paperwork is a core problem in cybersecurity assurance. ControlSift investigates whether parameter-efficient fine-tuning (QLoRA) of Gemma 3 1B Instruct improves five-class evidence-quality classification—SUFFICIENT, PARTIAL, INSUFFICIENT, IRRELEVANT, and CONTRADICTORY— on a leakage-controlled synthetic benchmark. The project freezes an evaluation protocol before large-model claims, reports classical baselines that establish task difficulty, and publishes only machine-readable metrics. On dataset v1.1.0, majority-class macro F1 is 0.067 and TF-IDF + logistic regression reaches approximately 0.53 on the sealed test set (0.52 on the challenge set). Gemma zero-shot, few-shot, and QLoRA results remain unavailable until free-GPU execution completes. Beyond the modeling ladder, ControlSift contributes a public research site, Failure Lab, and assurance documentation aligned with UN Sustainable Development Goal 16 (strong institutions) through more honest evidence triage.

    1. Introduction

    Security and governance, risk, and compliance (GRC) programs depend on evidence that controls operate as stated. In practice, binders fill with policies, screenshots, and exports that may be incomplete, off-objective, stale, or actively contradictory. Human reviewers can make these distinctions, but the volume of artifacts creates pressure to accept plausible paperwork as proof.

    Small instruction-tuned language models are an attractive assistive technology: they are cheap to adapt, portable across free GPU environments, and capable of structured classification when the task is well specified. Adaptation gains are only meaningful, however, when they exceed strong classical baselines and survive a sealed evaluation design. ControlSift therefore asks a concrete research question:

    Can parameter-efficient fine-tuning materially improve a small general-purpose language model’s ability to evaluate the sufficiency and relevance of cybersecurity control evidence?

    This paper walks the full project for residency review: motivation and societal framing, benchmark construction, modeling and evaluation design, classical results, responsible-AI controls, curriculum coverage, and the remaining GPU-dependent stages.

    2. Background and motivation

    Evidence quality is not the same as document relevance. A policy that requires multi-factor authentication is not proof that privileged accounts are enrolled. An export that shows an active privileged account without MFA can contradict the control. Reviewers therefore need labels finer than “related” versus “unrelated.”

    ControlSift adopts five classes used throughout the project:

    • SUFFICIENT — complete, timely, on-objective operational proof for the stated control.
    • PARTIAL — relevant operational content with material population or scope gaps.
    • INSUFFICIENT — on-topic material that fails as execution proof (policy, attestation, procedure, or unverifiable capture).
    • IRRELEVANT — off-objective substance filed under the control’s binder metadata.
    • CONTRADICTORY — otherwise complete framing that contains a control-failure fact.

    The modeling path follows a transparent ladder: majority class, TF-IDF logistic regression, Gemma 3 1B zero-shot prompting, few-shot prompting, and QLoRA adaptation. Each rung produces machine-readable metrics so improvements can be inspected rather than asserted.

    3. Problem formulation

    Formally, each case provides a control statement, environment, evidence type, and evidence text. The model must emit exactly one label from the five-class set. Evaluation uses macro F1 as the primary metric because the classes are balanced by construction and equally important for assurance practice.

    Hypotheses locked in the research protocol:

    • H1 — QLoRA outperforms fixed zero-shot prompting on sealed test macro F1.
    • H2 — QLoRA outperforms fixed five-class few-shot prompting.
    • H3 — Gains remain visible on the challenge set of held-out scenario families.
    • H4 — Performance increases with training-set size, with diminishing returns.
    • H5 — Difficult boundaries remain, especially among PARTIAL, SUFFICIENT, INSUFFICIENT, and IRRELEVANT.

    Negative or mixed findings are treated as valid scientific outcomes.

    4. Societal alignment

    Under Mentor Me Collective’s UN Sustainable Development Goal project-build model, ControlSift maps primarily to SDG 16 (Peace, justice and strong institutions). Institutions that certify controls with weak evidence erode accountability. A model that helps reviewers separate proof from paperwork—and that is bounded by explicit prohibited uses—supports more honest assurance practice (targets 16.6 and 16.10).

    Supporting alignments include SDG 9, through methods designed for free-tier GPU reproduction (QLoRA / 4-bit quantization), and SDG 4, through a public, curriculum-visible research artifact rather than a private homework dump. Detail lives on the UN SDG alignment page.

    5. Benchmark design

    5.1 Corpus

    The benchmark contains 1,500 synthetic cases across 13 control domains, balanced at 300 examples per class. Splits are train 1,000 / validation 200 / test 200 / challenge 100. Scenario families are assigned wholly to one split so near-duplicate mutations cannot leak across partitions. Generation is deterministic under seed 42.

    5.2 Representation

    Version 1.1 packs each case as a compositional evidence packet: dual sections containing claim-like text and a near-domain distractor, a substance pointer, shared decoy lexicon, identical SCOPE scaffolding (inventory versus file_rows), epistemic hedges where appropriate, and a ROW_DETAIL line. Class signal is compositional rather than a single cue phrase per label. This design reduces trivial bag-of-words separability while remaining rule-derivable for integrity checks.

    5.3 Integrity gates

    Continuous integration enforces schema validity, class balance, and family non-leakage. A lexical-ceiling test requires TF-IDF test macro F1 to remain below a triviality threshold. Protocol seal hashes for all splits are recorded in governance/PROTOCOL_SEAL.json under tag protocol-v1-locked.

    6. Methods

    6.1 Classical baselines

    The majority-class baseline predicts the training majority label. TF-IDF (unigrams and bigrams) with class-balanced logistic regression establishes a strong lexical floor. Inputs concatenate control statement, evidence type, and evidence text.

    6.2 Language-model ladder

    The primary model is google/gemma-3-1b-it. Zero-shot evaluation uses a frozen canonical prompt that requests a single label and short rationale as JSON. Few-shot evaluation adds one fixed representative example per class drawn only from train and validation. Domain adaptation uses QLoRA (4-bit NF4, LoRA rank 16, α 16) with target modules resolved from the loaded architecture. Malformed generations parse to UNPARSEABLE and count against performance.

    6.3 Training and compute

    Training and inference are packaged for free GPU environments (Kaggle / Colab). A smoke configuration validates the path before full runs. Secrets remain in platform secret stores; they are never committed to the repository.

    7. Evaluation protocol

    Evaluation is sealed before final LLM claims. The protocol freezes hypotheses, primary metric, model identity, prompt module, and split hashes. Inference is deterministic where supported. Reported metrics include accuracy, macro precision/recall, per-class F1, confusion matrices, challenge macro F1, and parse-success rate. Bootstrap confidence intervals and paired tests are available once multiple model prediction files exist.

    Public results are copied only from results/*/metrics_*.json into docs/data/results.json. Missing experiments appear as null values rather than zeros.

    8.2 Interpretation

    The classical stage already answers a methodological question: a sealed synthetic evidence benchmark can be constructed so that lexical shortcuts do not collapse the research agenda. Whether QLoRA recovers additional semantic headroom is the remaining empirical claim and will be reported from GPU artifacts without placeholder scores.

    9. Error analysis

    On the sealed test set, TF-IDF per-class F1 is strongest for CONTRADICTORY (approximately 0.93) and weakest for SUFFICIENT (approximately 0.25), with PARTIAL and IRRELEVANT in between. Residual confusion concentrates on compositional coverage and substance-pointer distinctions—precisely the boundaries human reviewers debate.

    The public Failure Lab presents concrete cases drawn from TF-IDF disagreements, with slots for future Gemma predictions. Detailed notes appear in the project error-analysis report.

    10. Responsible innovation

    ControlSift is a research benchmark, not an automated auditor. Intended use is method comparison and study of assistive triage under synthetic conditions. Prohibited uses include production pass/fail scoring of customer evidence without human review and marketing of synthetic scores as compliance certification.

    Governance artifacts include a Data Card, Model Card, AI risk register, limitations statement, intended-use policy, and label-audit protocol. Collectively they address automation bias, data leakage, overclaiming, and provenance of labels. The Assurance hub aggregates these documents for review.

    11. Curriculum demonstration

    The project implements the DeepMind AI Research Foundations pathway as an end-to-end research artifact: language-model task framing; data representation; training and generalization discipline; transformer base model; fine-tuning via QLoRA; acceleration through quantization and free-GPU packaging; alignment-adjacent controls through parsing, prohibited uses, and risk documentation; and Capstone Course 8 through this paper and the surrounding evaluator pathway. A course-by-course table is available on the curriculum map.

    12. Limitations

    • Synthetic packets are not multi-document binders and do not include OCR noise or organizational politics.
    • Labels are rule-derived with audit coverage; they are not a large-scale human gold standard.
    • Compositional scaffolding may reward schema reading; that risk is disclosed and will be monitored in LLM error analysis.
    • Class difficulty is uneven for classical models; macro F1 must be read with per-class tables.
    • Gemma conclusions are unavailable until GPU result files exist.
    • Results for Gemma 3 1B do not automatically transfer to other model families or scales.

    13. Reproducibility

    The repository includes the generator, validators, baseline scripts, evaluation aggregation, figure builders, tests, and a secret-safe Kaggle packaging path. Classical reproduction: 

    pip install -e ".[dev]"
python scripts/validate_dataset.py
python scripts/run_tfidf_baseline.py
python scripts/run_evaluation.py
pytest

    GPU execution follows notebooks/KAGGLE_SAFE_RUN.md and notebooks/kaggle_runner.ipynb, writing metrics under results/gemma_* and refreshing the public results file.

    14. Future work

    Immediate next steps are Gemma zero-shot, few-shot, and QLoRA evaluation on free GPU; statistical comparison against classical baselines; Failure Lab updates with model disagreements; and completion of hypotheses H1–H5 in the research report. If schema gaming dominates LLM behavior, a prose-hardened dataset revision can be opened under a new protocol tag without rewriting the classical-stage record.

    15. Conclusion

    ControlSift establishes a sealed, family-isolated evidence-quality benchmark and shows that careful construction can prevent lexical baselines from saturating the task. With majority macro F1 at 0.067 and TF-IDF near 0.53, the classical stage leaves measurable headroom for language-model adaptation. Whether QLoRA fills that headroom is the open empirical question. Until those runs complete, the project’s contribution is already concrete: an inspectable research process that privileges integrity over premature model scores—and that frames small-model assistance as a tool for stronger institutional honesty, not a replacement for human judgment.
