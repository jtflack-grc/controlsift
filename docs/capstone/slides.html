    Slide 01 / 08 - Title

    ControlSift

    Can a small language model help under-resourced teams tell proof from paperwork?

    • MMC / Google DeepMind AI Research Foundations capstone
    • MMC rubric option 4: Reduced Inequalities / official UN SDG 10
    • Synthetic research artifact - not a production auditor

    Slide 02 / 08 - Problem

    Unequal access to assurance capacity

    • Control assessment requires evidence that implementation and outcomes can be verified
    • Small organizations often face limited cybersecurity budgets and specialist capacity
    • Policy documents can be relevant without proving control operation
    • Goal: open evidence-triage research, not automated certification

    Slide 03 / 08 - Aim

    Research aim

    • Five evidence-quality classes: sufficient, partial, insufficient, irrelevant, contradictory
    • Primary metric: macro F1; seed 42; family-isolated splits
    • Classical path: majority -> TF-IDF
    • Gemma path: zero-shot -> few-shot -> QLoRA

    Slide 04 / 08 - Method

    Method and version boundary

    • Classical experiments use hardened dataset v1.1.0
    • Gemma zero-shot, few-shot, and QLoRA use dataset v1.0.0
    • Gemma 3 1B; QLoRA with 4-bit NF4 on free-tier GPU
    • Cross-version scores are descriptive, not a controlled head-to-head comparison

    Slide 05 / 08 - Results

    Published results

    • v1.1 classical: majority 0.0667; TF-IDF 0.5327 (challenge 0.5237)
    • v1.0 Gemma: zero-shot 0.0796; few-shot 0.1365; QLoRA 0.0827
    • Within Gemma v1.0, few-shot is strongest
    • QLoRA parse success is only 0.435; output reliability is a first-class failure mode

    Slide 06 / 08 - Responsible AI

    Research integrity and responsible use

    • Synthetic data only; no customer evidence
    • Data Card, Model Card, Risk Register, Intended Use, Failure Lab
    • Malformed generations count as errors; no invented metrics
    • No claim that v1.1 classical scores definitively beat v1.0 Gemma on the same benchmark

    Slide 07 / 08 - Impact

    Practicality, sustainability, and impact

    • Open method and synthetic data reduce privacy and cost barriers
    • Classical baseline is inexpensive; Gemma work uses free or commodity GPU paths
    • Useful as a research and training artifact for human reviewers
    • Production use would require governed real-world validation and stronger output controls
