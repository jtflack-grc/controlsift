    Full speaking script (~4:45)

    Read aloud exactly as written. ~700 words at a calm ~145 wpm ≈ 4:45. Slide cues in brackets are not spoken.

    0:00–0:40 · Hook · [Slide 01]
    “Hello — I’m John Flack. This is ControlSift, my Mentor Me Collective DeepMind AI Research Foundations capstone. The official Sustainable Development Goal is Goal 10, Reduced Inequalities. The research question is simple: can a small language model help teams with less GRC budget tell proof from paperwork? ControlSift is a sealed synthetic research protocol. It is not a production auditor, and it does not score customer binders.”
    0:40–1:25 · Problem · [Slide 02]
    “High-quality assurance review is expensive and unequally distributed. Smaller clinics, schools, and nonprofits often inherit binders that look compliant when controls do not actually operate. A written MFA policy is not proof that privileged accounts are enrolled. An export that shows a privileged user without MFA can contradict the control outright. That inequality — unequal access to honest evidence triage — is what I’m targeting. Not by replacing auditors, but by publishing an open methods study that under-resourced reviewers can inspect.”
    1:25–2:20 · Aim & methods · [Slides 03–04]
    “The aim is five-class evidence-quality classification on a sealed synthetic benchmark: Sufficient, Partial, Insufficient, Irrelevant, and Contradictory. Primary metric is macro F1. Seed forty-two. Protocol tag protocol-v-one-locked — hypotheses frozen before large-model claims.

    Methods: one thousand five hundred compositional evidence packets, family-isolated splits so near-duplicates cannot leak across train, validation, test, and challenge. The modeling ladder is majority class, then TF-IDF logistic regression, then Gemma 3 one-B instruction-tuned zero-shot, few-shot, and QLoRA on a free-tier GPU path. Malformed model outputs count as errors — we do not remap them to a preferred label. Dataset version one-point-one hardened the packets after an early draft let bag-of-words cheat.”
    2:20–3:40 · Results · [Slide 05]
    “Here are the sealed test results — two hundred cases each — spoken from the published result files.

    Majority-class macro F1 is zero point zero six six seven. TF-IDF still leads at zero point five three two seven — about fifty-three percent — with challenge macro F1 zero point five two three seven. Gemma 3 one-B zero-shot is zero point zero seven nine six, with parse success about seventy-six percent. Few-shot is the best of the Gemma ladder at zero point one three six five, with parse success ninety-eight point five percent. QLoRA lands at zero point zero eight two seven — and label parse success is only about forty-three point five percent, so that score is fragile.

    Honest takeaway: lexical TF-IDF wins this benchmark. Few-shot is the best small-LLM rung. QLoRA did not beat few-shot on test macro F1. Negative findings stay in the record. Every number comes from machine-readable metrics — no invented scores.”
    3:40–4:20 · Responsible AI · [Slides 06–07]
    “Responsible innovation: synthetic data only, a Data Card, a Risk Register, a Failure Lab for hard cases, and a clear Intended Use — research and education, not automated certification. Human judgment remains authoritative. The curriculum map shows the DeepMind path end to end, including prompting, LoRA, and free-tier acceleration. Impact under Goal 10 is access: open, free-tier-reproducible triage methods so smaller organizations are not locked out of rigorous assurance craft.”
    4:20–4:55 · Close · [Slide 08]
    “Status today: research metrics, report and slides PDFs, Week-three badge evidence, and the public GitHub Pages site are done. The only remaining submit step is this recording — the ≤5-minute video — then form upload with the report and slides. Thank you for reviewing ControlSift.”
