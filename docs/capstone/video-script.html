    Full speaking script

    0:00-0:35 - Slide 01
    Hello, I'm John Flack. This is ControlSift, my Mentor Me Collective and Google DeepMind AI Research Foundations capstone. The project asks a simple question: can a small language model help under-resourced teams tell real security proof from paperwork? For the MMC rubric, the project uses option 4, Reduced Inequalities; the official United Nations designation is Sustainable Development Goal 10. ControlSift is a synthetic research artifact, not a production auditor.
    0:35-1:15 - Slide 02
    The problem is evidence quality. A policy that requires multi-factor authentication may be relevant, but it does not prove that privileged accounts were actually enrolled during the assessment period. NIST control-assessment guidance focuses on verifying implementation and outcomes, while NIST small-business guidance also recognizes that smaller organizations often operate with limited cybersecurity resources and budgets. ControlSift studies whether low-cost evidence triage methods can help without lowering the assurance standard or replacing human judgment.
    1:15-2:05 - Slides 03-04
    The task has five labels: Sufficient, Partial, Insufficient, Irrelevant, and Contradictory. Macro F1 is the primary metric, seed forty-two, with scenario families isolated across train, validation, test, and challenge splits. The classical experiments use the hardened dataset version one-point-one. The Gemma zero-shot, few-shot, and QLoRA experiments were completed earlier on dataset version one-point-zero using a free-tier GPU path. Because those versions differ, the cross-version scores are descriptive rather than a controlled head-to-head comparison.
    2:05-3:25 - Slide 05
    Here are the published results. On dataset version one-point-one, the majority baseline reaches macro F1 zero point zero six six seven, and TF-IDF logistic regression reaches zero point five three two seven, with challenge macro F1 zero point five two three seven. On dataset version one-point-zero, Gemma zero-shot reaches zero point zero seven nine six. Gemma few-shot reaches zero point one three six five and is the strongest Gemma run. QLoRA reaches zero point zero eight two seven and does not beat few-shot. Its label parse-success rate is only about forty-three point five percent, which is itself an important failure mode. I do not claim that the v1.1 classical result definitively beats the v1.0 Gemma results on the same benchmark, because they were not run on the same dataset version.
    3:25-4:10 - Slides 06-07
    Responsible use is part of the project, not an appendix. The data is synthetic. The site includes a Data Card, Model Card, Risk Register, Intended Use, Failure Lab, and explicit claim boundaries. Malformed model outputs count as errors, and the negative QLoRA result stays in the record. The work is also designed to be reproducible at low cost: the classical baseline is inexpensive, and the small-model experiments use free or commodity GPU infrastructure. Production use would require governed real-world validation, security review, stronger output controls, and accountable human oversight.
    4:10-4:45 - Slide 08
    At this point the research runs, results, report, slides, sources, assurance artifacts, curriculum map, and public GitHub Pages site are complete. The only remaining capstone deliverable is this video and the final form upload. Thank you for reviewing ControlSift.
