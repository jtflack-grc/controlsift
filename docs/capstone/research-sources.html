ControlSift

    Research sources & claim boundaries

    ControlSift's model scores come from its own sealed synthetic benchmark. The external sources below do something different: they ground the real-world control-assessment problem, the resource-access motivation, the model and fine-tuning choices, and the project's human-oversight boundaries.

    Research-integrity rule No outside source is used to imply that ControlSift works in production. ControlSift-specific performance claims come only from results/* and docs/data/results.json. External literature supports context and method selection, not the experiment's outcome.

    Problem and assurance grounding

    NIST · Control assessment

    NIST SP 800-53A Rev. 5 — Assessing Security and Privacy Controls

    Supports: Control assessment is a structured process for determining whether controls are implemented and achieving intended outcomes. NIST provides customizable assessment procedures and guidance for analyzing assessment results.

    Does not prove: that a language model can perform control assessment, or that ControlSift's five labels are a NIST taxonomy.

    NIST publication page · DOI

    NIST · Assessment outcomes

    NIST release note for SP 800-53A Rev. 5

    Supports: NIST describes assessment as verifying that controls are implemented, meet stated control objectives, and achieve desired security and privacy outcomes. That distinction motivates ControlSift's focus on operational proof rather than document relevance alone.

    Does not prove: that every policy document is insufficient evidence; evidence sufficiency depends on the specific assessment objective and context.

    NIST release

    NIST · Resource constraints

    NIST Small Business Cybersecurity Corner

    Supports: NIST explicitly recognizes that smaller businesses operate with limited resources and budgets and need practical, actionable, cost-effective cybersecurity guidance and solutions.

    Does not prove: that small organizations specifically lack evidence-review expertise. ControlSift interprets broader cybersecurity resource constraints as a reason to study lower-cost assurance-support methods.

    NIST Small Business Cybersecurity Corner

    NIST · Workforce constraints

    Building Your Small Business' Cybersecurity Team

    Supports: NIST notes that small businesses often face limited resources, may be unable to afford dedicated cybersecurity staff, and may need outsourcing, upskilling, or community support.

    Does not prove: that AI should replace scarce professionals. ControlSift explicitly keeps the human reviewer authoritative.

    Model and method grounding

    Academic paper · PEFT

    Dettmers et al. — QLoRA: Efficient Finetuning of Quantized LLMs

    Supports: The QLoRA method used in ControlSift: gradients are backpropagated through a frozen 4-bit quantized language model into low-rank adapters, reducing memory requirements for fine-tuning. The paper introduces NF4 and other memory-saving techniques.

    Does not prove: that QLoRA should improve this cybersecurity evidence task. ControlSift tests that hypothesis rather than assuming it.

    Dettmers, Pagnoni, Holtzman & Zettlemoyer (2023) · arXiv:2305.14314

    Google DeepMind · Model documentation

    Gemma 3 model card and technical report

    Supports: Gemma 3 is a family of lightweight open models, including a 1B text model. Google's documentation characterizes the 1B model as suitable for small applications and constrained environments, which is consistent with ControlSift's low-resource research design.

    Does not prove: domain suitability for cybersecurity evidence assessment. The model card's general benchmark performance is not substituted for ControlSift's task-specific results.

    Responsible-AI and human-oversight grounding

    NIST · AI risk management

    NIST AI RMF Core and Playbook

    Supports: NIST calls for defined human-AI roles and responsibilities, documented human oversight, scoped use, risk controls, and measurement of AI-system behavior and downstream outcomes.

    Does not prescribe: ControlSift's exact governance files. The project's Data Card, Model Card, risk register, intended-use boundary, and human-review requirement are implementation choices informed by the same risk-management principles.

    NIST · Generative AI profile

    NIST AI 600-1 — Generative AI Profile

    Supports: Lifecycle risk management and trustworthiness considerations for generative-AI systems, including design, development, use, and evaluation.

    Does not classify: ControlSift as a production generative-AI system. The project uses the profile as responsible-AI context for a bounded research artifact.

    Societal framing

    United Nations · Sustainable Development Goals

    UN Sustainable Development Goal 10 — Reduced Inequalities

    Supports: The official UN designation: Reduced Inequalities is SDG 10. ControlSift's capstone maps its low-cost, open research design to the program's Reduced Inequalities option.

    Does not claim: that the UN specifically identifies cybersecurity evidence review as an SDG 10 target. The cybersecurity-assurance connection is the project's capstone interpretation.

    How these sources connect to ControlSift

    Problem statement
    NIST control-assessment guidance establishes that assurance is about whether controls are implemented and achieve intended outcomes, not merely whether related documentation exists.
    Access motivation
    NIST small-business guidance documents real cybersecurity resource and staffing constraints. ControlSift studies whether a low-cost research method could contribute useful triage knowledge without pretending to replace expertise.
    Technical design
    QLoRA literature and Gemma documentation ground the choice of a memory-efficient fine-tuning method and a lightweight 1B model. Their claims are not treated as evidence of task-specific success.
    Responsible use
    NIST AI RMF materials support explicit scope, oversight, documentation, and accountability. ControlSift therefore publishes limitations and keeps human judgment authoritative.
    Outcome evidence
    Only ControlSift's sealed experiment files support statements such as “TF-IDF leads” or “QLoRA did not beat few-shot.”
