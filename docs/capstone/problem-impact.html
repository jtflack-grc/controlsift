ControlSift

    Problem, aim, stakeholders, impact

    Capstone Course 8 framing without diluting ControlSift’s research identity.

    Problem statement

    Cybersecurity and GRC teams must distinguish artifacts that substantively demonstrate control operation from evidence that is incomplete, irrelevant, stale, or contradictory. Confusing a policy PDF with proof produces false assurance — an institutional failure mode, not a UX annoyance.

    Research aim

    Investigate whether domain adaptation using QLoRA improves Gemma 3 1B IT on a bounded five-class evidence-quality task, under a sealed evaluation protocol, with classical baselines and explicit failure analysis.

    Primary metric
    Macro F1 (test + challenge)
    Hypotheses
    H1–H5 frozen in Protocol
    Surface
    Compositional evidence packets (v1.1) — disclosed, not oversold as binder OCR

    Stakeholders

    • Primary beneficiaries (aspirational) — human evidence reviewers in under-resourced assurance teams who need triage aids, not autopilots.
    • Direct users of this artifact — ML / RAI reviewers, recruiters, MMC evaluators reading the public site.
    • At risk if misused — organizations that might treat model scores as audit conclusions (prohibited).
    • Not stakeholders we optimize for — SaaS buyers, live inference customers, document-ingestion pipelines (out of v1 scope).

    Intended impact

    Explore whether small, specialized language models could eventually assist human reviewers in evidence triage while explicitly studying risks of false assurance and inappropriate automation — aligned with SDG 16 institutional accountability.

    Prohibited impact (by design)

    • Automated pass/fail on customer controls without human review
    • Marketing “AI auditor” claims from synthetic-benchmark scores
    • Quiet prompt tuning against the sealed test set
