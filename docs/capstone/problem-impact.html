ControlSift

    Problem & impact

    Research problem, aim, stakeholders, and intended outcomes.

    Problem statement

    Cybersecurity and GRC teams must distinguish artifacts that demonstrate control operation from evidence that is incomplete, irrelevant, stale, or contradictory. Treating a policy document as proof produces false assurance and weakens institutional accountability.

    Research aim

    Determine whether domain adaptation with QLoRA improves Gemma 3 1B IT on a five-class evidence-quality task, using sealed evaluation, classical baselines, and explicit failure analysis.

    Primary metric
    Macro F1 on sealed test and challenge splits
    Hypotheses
    H1–H5 in the research protocol
    Benchmark
    Synthetic compositional evidence packets (dataset v1.1.0)

    Stakeholders

    • Intended beneficiaries — human evidence reviewers who need triage support, not autopilots.
    • Readers of this work — ML and responsible-AI reviewers, hiring managers, and residency evaluators.
    • Protected parties — organizations that must not treat model scores as audit conclusions.

    Intended outcomes

    A measured answer to whether small-model adaptation can assist evidence triage, plus a public record of remaining failure modes — official GCLP goal SDG 10 (unequal access to trustworthy assurance), with institutional honesty as the mechanism of impact.

    Out of scope

    • Automated pass/fail decisions on customer controls without human review
    • Claims that synthetic-benchmark scores certify production control effectiveness
    • Prompt changes tuned against the sealed test set after protocol lock
    Intended use UN SDG alignment
